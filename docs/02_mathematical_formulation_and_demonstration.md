## 2. Dimensional convention and canonical equation

To avoid the dimensional inconsistency detected in early versions, a single convention is adopted in which ω carries the temporal scale of the system:

$$
\frac{dS}{dt}=\omega\left[\sigma S-\chi S^2+\kappa S^{2+\delta}\right]
$$

with $[\omega]=T^{-1}$, $[\sigma]=1$, $[\chi]=S^{-1}$ and $[\kappa]=S^{-(1+\delta)}$. Thus, every term on the right-hand side has dimension $S\,T^{-1}$.

Defining $u=S/S_0$ and $\tau=t/t_0$, with

$$
t_0=\frac{1}{\omega\sigma},
$$

the dimensionless canonical equation is

$$
\frac{du}{d\tau}=u-\hat\chi u^2+\hat\kappa u^{2+\delta}.
$$

- $u=S/S_0$: normalized structural state.
- $\tau=t/t_0$: dimensionless time.
- $\hat\chi=\chi S_0/\sigma$: effective environmental containment, saturation or dissipation.
- $\hat\kappa=\kappa S_0^{1+\delta}/\sigma$: structural self-interaction or reinforcement.
- $\delta>0$ in the general bifurcation analysis developed in this Part II.

This equation is not intended by itself to represent all of TSP. It initially formalizes the competition among growth, containment and self-reinforcement.

## 3. Minimal canonical case

For $\delta=1$, $\hat\chi=2.5$ and $\hat\kappa=1$:

$$
\frac{du}{d\tau}=u-2.5u^2+u^3=u(u-0.5)(u-2).
$$

The exact fixed points are:

- $u_0^*=0$: unstable repeller.
- $u_1^*=0.5$: stable attractor.
- $u_2^*=2.0$: unstable fixed point and dynamical separatrix.

$$
u_0<2\Rightarrow u(\tau)\to0.5
$$

$$
u_0=2\Rightarrow \text{unstable separatrix}
$$

$$
u_0>2\Rightarrow \text{prevalence regime}
$$

## 4. Exact implicit solution and critical times

$$
\frac{1}{u(u-0.5)(u-2)}=\frac1u-\frac{4/3}{u-0.5}+\frac{1/3}{u-2}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\tau(u)=\ln\left|\frac{u}{u_0}\right|-\frac43\ln\left|\frac{u-0.5}{u_0-0.5}\right|+\frac13\ln\left|\frac{u-2}{u_0-2}\right|.
$$

For $u_0>2$, defining

$$
F(u)=\ln u-\frac43\ln(u-0.5)+\frac13\ln(u-2),
$$

we have $F(u)\to0$ as $u\to\infty$, and therefore

$$
\tau_c(u_0)=-F(u_0).
$$

Reference values:

$$
\tau_c(2.2)\approx0.455526,\qquad \tau_c(3.0)\approx0.123109.
$$

These values replace earlier preliminary estimates. Mathematical divergence marks the limit of validity of the effective continuous description; it must not automatically be identified with an infinite physical magnitude.

## 6. Asymptotic scaling law and testing

In the asymptotic prevalence regime, when the superlinear term dominates,

$$
\frac{dS}{dt}\approx\omega\kappa S^{2+\delta},
$$

integration gives

$$
t_c=\frac{1}{\omega\kappa(1+\delta)S_0^{1+\delta}},
$$

and hence

$$
t_c\propto S_0^{-(1+\delta)}.
$$

This law is not presented as exclusive to TSP. It is a prediction of this realization of the model in its asymptotic region. Systematic absence of the slope $-(1+\delta)$ where the regime assumptions are valid would falsify this specific realization.

## 7. From the particular case to the general criterion

Write

$$
\frac{du}{d\tau}=u\,h(u),
$$

with

$$
h(u)=1-\hat\chi u+\hat\kappa u^{1+\delta}.
$$

For $\hat\chi>0$, $\hat\kappa>0$ and $\delta>0$,

$$
u_{\min}=\left[\frac{\hat\chi}{(1+\delta)\hat\kappa}\right]^{1/\delta}.
$$

The critical bifurcation occurs when $h(u_{\min})=0$, giving

$$
\hat\kappa_c=\frac{\delta^\delta\hat\chi^{1+\delta}}{(1+\delta)^{1+\delta}}.
$$

Define the **Prevalence Control Invariant**:

$$
\Theta=\frac{\delta^\delta}{(1+\delta)^{1+\delta}}\frac{\hat\chi^{1+\delta}}{\hat\kappa}.
$$

Its classification is:

| Condition | Topology | Result |
|---|---|---|
| $\Theta>1$ | Two positive fixed points | Stable attractor + unstable separatrix |
| $\Theta=1$ | Double root | Saddle-node bifurcation; semistable point |
| $\Theta<1$ | No positive fixed points | Global prevalence |

## 8. Verification of the general criterion

For $\hat\chi=2.5$ and $\delta=1$:

$$
\hat\kappa_c=\frac{\hat\chi^2}{4}=1.5625.
$$

| $\hat\kappa$ | $\Theta$ | Positive roots | Regime |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1.0000 | 1.562500 > 1 | 0.5 and 2.0 | Containment + threshold |
| 1.5625 | 1.000000 | 0.8, double root | Critical state |
| 2.0000 | 0.781250 < 1 | None | Global prevalence |

## 11. Structural memory: minimal formulation

The Base Document states that accumulated history modifies future possibilities. To represent this explicitly, one additional variable is introduced:

$$
m(\tau)=\text{accumulated structural memory}
$$

$$
\frac{dm}{d\tau}=u-\lambda m,
$$

where $\lambda>0$ represents loss or dissipation of the trace. Its solution is

$$
m(\tau)=m_0e^{-\lambda\tau}+\int_0^\tau e^{-\lambda(\tau-s)}u(s)\,ds.
$$

By construction, $m$ contains information about the previous trajectory of $u$. To make that memory dynamically active, the minimal extension is

$$
\hat\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}=\hat\kappa(1+\alpha m),
$$

$$
\frac{du}{d\tau}=u-\hat\chi u^2+\hat\kappa(1+\alpha m)u^{2+\delta},
$$

$$
\frac{dm}{d\tau}=u-\lambda m.
$$

**STRUCTURE → MEMORY → MODIFIED REINFORCEMENT → FUTURE TRAJECTORY**

## 12. Prevalence Memory Criterion

Memory directly modifies the control invariant:

$$
\Theta_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{\Theta_0}{1+\alpha m}.
$$

If $\Theta_0>1$, the memory value that reaches the threshold $\Theta_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$ is

$$
m_c=\frac{\Theta_0-1}{\alpha}.
$$

For the canonical case, with $\Theta_0=1.5625$ and $\alpha=1$:

$$
m_c=0.5625.
$$

## 13. Minimal test: same present, different history

Compare two systems with the same current structural state $u=1$ and the same parameters $\hat\chi=2.5$, $\hat\kappa=1$, $\delta=1$, and $\alpha=1$. Only memory differs.

| System | $u$ | $m$ | $\hat\kappa_{eff}$ | $\Theta_{eff}$ | $du/d\tau$ at that instant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 1.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 | 1.5625 | −0.5 |
| B | 1.0 | 0.7 | 1.7 | ≈0.9191 | +0.2 |

**SAME OBSERVABLE STATE $u$ + DIFFERENT MEMORY $m$ ⇒ DIFFERENT FUTURE**

This is the minimal verification of the intended principle: incorporated history ceases to be a passive record and modifies the future evolution of the enlarged system.

## 14. Figure 3 — Memory modifies the future trajectory

Two systems start from the same observable state $u_0=1$, with $\hat\chi=2.5$, $\hat\kappa=1$, $\delta=1$, $\alpha=1$ and $\lambda=2$, but with different initial memory. With $m_0=0$ the trajectory remains stabilizing; with $m_0=0.7$ effective reinforcement increases and the trajectory enters prevalence.

The simulation does not demonstrate that this is the only possible representation of physical memory. It demonstrates that the proposed minimal formulation is mathematically consistent with the TSP principle and produces a differentiable and verifiable consequence.

## 16. What TSP 1.0 demonstrates and does not demonstrate

Part II demonstrates mathematical properties of the proposed family of models: existence of attractors and separatrices, topological transition through $\Theta$, an asymptotic law in the dominant regime, and a minimal extension in which accumulated memory modifies the future.

It does not yet demonstrate that these mechanisms are universal in nature, nor does it by itself identify $u$, $\hat\chi$, $\hat\kappa$, $\delta$, $\alpha$ or $\lambda$ with specific magnitudes in all physical systems. That stage requires concrete physical realizations, data and independent testing.

## 17. Closing assessment of the first mathematical formulation

Within the scope established for TSP 1.0, the accumulated development constitutes a first coherent, minimal and verifiable mathematical formulation supporting the essential dynamical principles of the Base Document.

**PREVALENCE + THRESHOLD + TRANSITION + ACTIVE MEMORY**

Closing this Part II does not mean that TSP is complete or universally validated. It means that an explicit, reproducible mathematical core now exists and is susceptible to testing.

From this master document onward, specific applications and future extensions must remain separate from the TSP 1.0 core in order to preserve simplicity, traceability and evaluability.
