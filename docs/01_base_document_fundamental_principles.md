# TSP — Theory of Structural Prevalence
## Base Document 0.1 — Fundamental Principles

**Author:** Jesus Paz

## 1. Statement

Modern physics describes numerous natural phenomena with extraordinary precision through theories such as Quantum Mechanics and General Relativity. Each explains a specific domain of reality with great success.

The Theory of Structural Prevalence (TSP) is not intended to replace these theories, but to explore whether a more general physical principle exists that can describe a common behavior present at all levels of organization of matter.

The central hypothesis of TSP is that the evolution of physical structures depends not only on the local laws governing each phenomenon, but also on the accumulated history of the system itself. That history modifies future possibilities of evolution, generating preferred trajectories that tend to reinforce themselves over time.

From this perspective, the formation of structures would not be a succession of independent events, but a continuous process of organization in which each state partially conditions the next.

## 2. Central idea

TSP proposes that there is a universal principle of organization according to which physical structures evolve along prevalence trajectories.

A structure that reaches sufficient stability modifies the conditions of the environment in which it exists, increasing or decreasing the probability of certain subsequent evolutions.

Consequently, the history of the system ceases to be merely a record of the past and becomes an active element of its future evolution.

## 3. Fundamental hypothesis

Every physical structure retains, directly or indirectly, information about its formation process.

That information influences the subsequent evolution of the system.

Structural memory therefore constitutes a general physical property of organized systems.

## 4. Principle of Prevalence

Structural configurations do not all possess the same capacity for persistence.

Configurations with greater stability tend to persist for longer periods and to favor the emergence of new structures compatible with them.

Natural evolution thus shows a tendency toward the consolidation of certain structural trajectories.

## 5. Universality

TSP proposes that this principle is scale-independent.

The same general behavior should be found:

- in microscopic processes;
- in chemical systems;
- in biological systems;
- in geological processes;
- in planetary structures;
- in stars;
- in galaxies;
- and at any other level of organization of matter.

The particular physical mechanisms may differ, but the organizing principle would be the same.

## 6. Structural evolution

Every structure would generally follow a dynamic sequence characterized by:

1. initial emergence;
2. slow growth;
3. progressive consolidation;
4. acceleration of organization;
5. modification of the environment;
6. stabilization, transformation, or disappearance.

The duration of each phase will depend on the nature of the system, but the general sequence would constitute a universal property.

## 7. Role of matter

Matter would not represent an isolated state, but a manifestation of a continuous process of energy organization.

Some material configurations are more stable than others.

This differential stability leaves a permanent imprint on the evolution of the universe and conditions the emergence of subsequent structures.

## 8. Scales

TSP proposes that processes observed in everyday life are slow versions of the same structural behavior that, under extreme conditions of density or energy, occurs much more rapidly.

From this perspective, very different phenomena could share the same fundamental dynamic pattern.

## 9. General predictions

### Prediction 1

Material configurations do not all possess the same structural stability.

Some configurations will tend systematically to prevail over others, leaving a cumulative imprint on the evolution of the universe.

### Prediction 2

The same general pattern of organization will appear in systems belonging to completely different physical scales.

The difference between them will be quantitative, not qualitative.

### Prediction 3

There are no physical systems completely devoid of memory.

Every system retains some form of structural information capable of influencing its future evolution.

## 10. Mathematical objective

The objective of the mathematical formulation of TSP is to describe quantitatively:

- the formation of structures;
- the emergence of structural memory;
- prevalence mechanisms;
- stability thresholds;
- transitions between dynamic regimes;
- and the common evolution of systems belonging to different physical scales.

## 11. Scientific objective

TSP seeks to answer a single fundamental question:

> **Is there a universal principle of structural organization capable of explaining, through the same dynamic logic, the evolution of matter from microscopic to cosmological scales, without replacing existing physical theories, but instead providing a common conceptual framework for interpreting them?**
